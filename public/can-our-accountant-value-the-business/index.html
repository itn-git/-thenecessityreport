Can our accountant value the business for our separation?

Short answerThey can give you a figure, though in a separation it often won't settle anything. The business's accountant usually works for one of you, has prepared the accounts the value would rest on, and may not hold the valuation training a family law matter calls for, so the other side has good reason to question the number.

The Necessity Report™ gives you an independent view first, for a fixed $997 including GST. It is not a valuation; it tells you whether one is worth paying for.

Why the business accountant is in an awkward spot

  • They have a client relationship. Usually with the person who runs the business, which makes it hard for the other partner to accept their view as neutral.
  • They prepared the figures. A valuation tests the accounts, including adjustments for private costs and owner's wages, and it is difficult to test your own work.
  • Tax accounts are not valuation accounts. Statements prepared to manage tax often understate the profit a buyer would look at, for perfectly ordinary reasons.

None of this says the accountant is wrong or acting improperly. It simply means their figure may not carry much weight when the two of you disagree.

When their figure may be enough

If you both trust the accountant, the business is simple, and the figure sits comfortably with what you each know about the business, an agreed value based on their advice can be a sensible, low-cost path. It is worth checking with your lawyer or mediator before relying on it.

Where an independent check helps

Where one of you is uneasy, the question is often not "what is it worth?" but "is this figure safe to rely on, or do we need a proper valuation?" That is the question The Necessity Report™ answers. I have no connection with either of you or the business's accountant, and I tell you in writing whether a valuation is warranted, not warranted, or can't be decided on the records, with the questions to ask if it can't.
