How much does a business valuation cost in a divorce?

Short answerIt depends mostly on what the valuation is for and how complicated the business is. A report for negotiation or mediation on a single, straightforward business costs a few thousand dollars; a report prepared for court, or one covering a company, a trust and related entities, costs considerably more.

Before spending that, The Necessity Report™ tells you whether a valuation is needed at all, for a fixed $997 including GST. It is not a valuation itself.

What drives the fee

  • What the report is for. A valuation used to help you negotiate or mediate can be shorter than one prepared as expert evidence for court, which has to meet the court's rules and may involve the valuer giving evidence.
  • How many trading entities are involved. A sole trader is one business to value; a trading company with a related property entity is two. A trustee company that simply holds the trust and does not trade usually adds little to the cost, since there is no separate business in it to value.
  • The state of the records. Clean, up-to-date accounts take less time than missing years, draft statements or a loan account nobody can explain.
  • How much the two of you disagree. Where the parties dispute the facts, the valuer usually has to deal with both versions, and that takes time.

Who pays?

Where both parties instruct one valuer together, the cost is often shared equally, though that is something to agree at the start rather than assume. Where one party instructs a valuer alone, that party usually pays, and the other side may want their own. Your lawyer or mediator can tell you what is usual in your situation, and whether the fee can come out of the property pool.

The cheapest valuation is the one you don't need

A quick, low-cost valuation can suit a simple business, though a cheap report on a complicated one can leave both of you arguing about the number anyway.
