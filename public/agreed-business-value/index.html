We agree on the business value. Do we still need a valuation?

Short answerNot necessarily. If you both understand the business and the figure, agreeing a value can save you thousands. The risk is agreeing on a number that one of you hasn't really been in a position to check, since a settlement is very hard to reopen later.

The Necessity Report™ is an independent check on whether your agreed figure is safe to rely on, for a fixed $997 including GST. It does not put a value on the business.

When agreeing is usually sensible

  • You have both seen the last few years of financial statements and tax returns.
  • The business is simple, often one person, a vehicle and some equipment.
  • The figure sits sensibly against what the business earns and owns.
  • Your lawyers or mediator are comfortable with how you got there.

When it's worth a second look first

  • Only one of you really knows how the business runs or where the money goes.
  • The figure came from the business's own accountant, or from a quick online estimate.
  • There is a company or family trust involved, or money owed between the business and either of you.
  • Income fell around the time of the separation, and nobody has explained why.

Agreement made with incomplete information can feel like progress and still leave one of you short, sometimes by a large amount. Equally, it can be exactly right, and spending thousands to confirm it would be a waste.

Where the Report fits

I look at the records you provide and tell you, in writing, whether a formal valuation is warranted, not warranted, or can't be decided on what you've given me. If the records support your figure being reasonable to rely on without a valuation, you'll know; if they raise questions, you'll know what to ask before you sign anything. You have the answer within three to five business days of my receiving the documents.

The Report is not for court, is not a valuation and is not legal advice.
