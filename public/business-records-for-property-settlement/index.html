What business records do I need for my property settlement?

Short answerUsually the last three years of financial statements and tax returns for the business, plus anything showing how money moves between the business and the people who own it. Part-years and missing years are common, so send what you have; gaps are themselves useful information.

For The Necessity Report™ ($997 including GST), these are the records I ask for. If you can't get them all, the Report tells you which missing ones matter most.

The core records

  • Financial statements (profit and loss, and balance sheet) for the last three financial years
  • Business tax returns for the same years
  • Business activity statements (BAS) for the most recent year
  • If there is one, read-only access to the accounting system, such as Xero or MYOB

If a company or family trust is involved

  • Financial statements and tax returns for each related entity, such as a trustee company, a property-holding company or a second business
  • The trust deed, and any trustee distribution minutes
  • Share registers or unit holder details

Records about money moving between the business and its owners

  • Loan account statements or ledgers for each owner or director
  • Personal tax returns for the people working in the business
  • Any leases, especially where the business rents premises from a related party
  • Vehicle and equipment finance agreements

A few practical points

  • Draft accounts are fine, if labelled as draft. I would rather see a draft than nothing.
  • Send what you have, not what you can find in six months. If the records are thin, that is often the answer in itself.
  • If you can't get the records at all, that's a disclosure issue for your lawyer or mediator to raise; see My ex won't give me the business accounts.
