How long does a business valuation take, and what happens next?

Short answerThe valuation itself often takes a few weeks once the valuer has the records, though getting the records together is usually what takes longest. Court valuations take longer again, because of the rules they have to meet and the questions each side can put to the valuer.

If you're not yet sure you need a valuation at all, The Necessity Report™ answers that in three to five business days, for a fixed $997 including GST. It is not a valuation.

What usually happens, step by step

  1. Deciding whether a valuation is needed. This is where most delays start, since people go back and forth without a clear answer. The Report is designed for this step.
  2. Choosing the valuer. Often one valuer instructed by both of you, which saves cost and argument.
  3. Agreeing the scope. Which entities are valued, at what date, and for what purpose (negotiation, mediation or court).
  4. Gathering the records. Financial statements, tax returns, loan accounts and the rest; see What business records do I need.
  5. The valuer's work. Reviewing the records, asking questions of both of you, and preparing the report.
  6. Using the result. Your lawyers or mediator work the value into the overall settlement.

What slows things down

  • Missing years of accounts, or accounts still in draft
  • More than one entity, such as a company owned by a trust
  • One party slow to provide information
  • Disagreement about the scope, or the date the business should be valued at

Where the Report fits

The Report covers step 1. You get, in writing, one of three answers: a valuation is warranted, not warranted, or can't be decided on the records. If it can't be decided, you get the documents most likely to settle it, and one free update if you send them together within 21 days. Either way, you go into the next step knowing why.

The Report is not for court, is not a valuation and is not legal advice.
