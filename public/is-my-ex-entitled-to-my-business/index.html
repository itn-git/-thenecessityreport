Is my ex entitled to a share of my business?

Short answerThe business usually forms part of the property pool, whoever runs it and whoever's name it is in. How the pool is divided is a legal question, decided on the circumstances of your relationship, and there is no automatic half share. What I can help with is the step before that: knowing what the business is actually worth having a view on.

The Necessity Report™ tells you whether a formal valuation is warranted, for a fixed $997 including GST. It does not value the business or say how property should be divided.

What I can say as an accountant

I'm not a lawyer, so the question of shares belongs with yours. From the numbers side, though, a few things are worth knowing:

  • "It's in my name" doesn't take it out of the pool. A business held through a company or trust is usually still considered, though how it is treated can depend on the structure.
  • "I owned it before we met" matters, but doesn't settle it. How that history is weighed is again a legal question, and the business's value at the relevant time still has to be known.
  • "My ex never worked in it" is about contributions, not value. Contributions to the family and the home are weighed too, so what the business is worth still matters.

If you own the business

It is natural to worry that a valuation will be used against you. An independent view can work in your favour as easily as against you, since it gives both of you a footing that isn't just one person's say-so. If the records show the business is mainly your own work and a set of tools, the Report will say so plainly.

If your ex owns the business

You may be the one who knows least about it. The Report gives you an independent read of the records you can get, and the questions to ask if they don't settle it.
