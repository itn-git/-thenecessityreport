What you are paying for

Most people facing a business in a property settlement are not accountants, and neither, usually, are the lawyers and mediators helping them. You may be weighing up whether to spend thousands on a valuation, or whether to accept a figure you can't really check. The Report takes that decision off your plate: I read the records, and you get an independent view from someone who understands how small businesses actually run.

One of three answers, in writing

  1. A formal valuation is warranted, with the main reasons, so you can instruct one with confidence.
  2. A formal valuation is not warranted, with the reasons, so you can move forward without that cost.
  3. It can't be decided on the records provided, with the documents most likely to help and the questions worth asking.

There is no value figure in the Report, since it is not a valuation. You can see the format for yourself in the sample Report, and the way I work through the records in How I decide.

Why the questions matter

Imagine the accounts show a modest business, but a few questions about a loan account and a related company lead to records showing it is worth several hundred thousand dollars more. That is the kind of difference the right questions can make, and it is very hard to ask them if you don't know what's missing.

If the answer is "can't tell"

The Report names the documents most likely to help. Send them in one batch within 21 days and I'll update the Report at no charge. The update may still say it can't be decided, since nobody knows in advance what new documents will show, and once that batch is reviewed the engagement is closed.

Ordering together

Many couples find it easier to order the Report jointly, so you both see the same independent answer. One of you places the order and pays, and the Report is addressed to both of you. How you share the cost is up to you.

Who it suits, and who it doesn't

  • It suits separating couples with a small or medium business where it isn't clear whether a valuation is worth the cost, and the lawyers and mediators advising them.
  • It isn't for matters already heading to court with a single expert appointed, or anyone who needs a value figure today; in that case you need a valuation, and my valuation fees are published.

What happens if a valuation is needed?

Any qualified valuer can do it. I can do it too, but only if both of you agree after seeing the Report.

The fine print, plainly

You order through one online form, tick a few boxes confirming you understand what the Report is and isn't, and pay. The fee is non-refundable once work starts.

The Report is not for court, is not a valuation and is not legal advice.
