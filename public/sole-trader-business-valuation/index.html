Does a sole trader business need valuing in a separation?

Short answerOften not as a business, though its assets still count. Many sole trader businesses are really the owner's own work under an ABN, and once you take out a fair wage for that work there may be little left that a buyer would pay for. The tools, vehicles, stock and money owed to the business are a different matter, since those have value whoever owns them.

The Necessity Report™ tells you whether a formal valuation is worth paying for in your situation, for a fixed $997 including GST. It does not value the business.

Signs the business is mainly the owner's job

  • Customers come because of the owner personally, and would leave if they did.
  • There are no employees, or only casual help.
  • Profit is roughly what the owner would earn doing the same work for someone else.

Signs there may be more to it

  • Profit is consistently well above a fair wage for the owner's hours.
  • There are staff, a workshop, contracts or repeat customers who would stay with a new owner.
  • The business has grown steadily, or carries a brand or licence with value of its own.
  • Business and personal money run through the same accounts, so the true profit isn't obvious.

Why "sole trader" doesn't settle it

Being a sole trader is a tax and legal setup, not a measure of value. I have seen sole traders whose businesses were worth very little beyond their equipment, and others that would sell for a solid sum. What decides it is what the records show about earnings, the owner's role and the assets, and that is what I look at.
