My ex won't give me the business accounts. What can I do?

Short answerGetting the records is a legal question, so start with your lawyer or mediator. In family law property matters both parties have a duty to disclose their financial information, and there are formal ways to ask for it. What I can help with is working out what the records you do have already show, and which missing ones matter most.

The Necessity Report™ works from whatever records you can provide, for a fixed $997 including GST. It does not value the business or replace the disclosure process.

First, the legal side (briefly)

I'm not a lawyer, so I won't tell you how to enforce disclosure. Your lawyer or mediator can explain your options and the timing that suits your matter. A clear, specific list of the documents you need tends to get a better response than a general request for "the accounts", which is where I come in.

What you may already have

People are often surprised by how much they already hold or can get without anyone's cooperation:

  • Joint personal tax returns or notices of assessment
  • Bank statements for joint accounts that the business paid into or out of
  • ASIC company extracts, which show directors and shareholders
  • Loan applications or finance documents that described the business's income
  • Emails, invoices or other business paperwork you were copied into

Missing records versus hidden ones

Not every gap means something is being hidden. Small businesses are often behind on their accounts, and some owners genuinely don't keep good records. The useful question is which missing documents would change the picture, since that tells you what to press for and what to let go.

Where the Report fits

I review what you can provide and tell you, in writing, whether a formal valuation is warranted, not warranted, or can't be decided on the records. If it can't be decided, the Report names the documents most likely to help, which gives your lawyer or mediator a specific list to ask for. If you send those documents in one batch within 21 days, I'll update the Report at no charge.

The Report is not for court, is not a valuation and is not legal advice.
